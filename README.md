This capstone project is to address public health disparities in the U.S. for cancer mortality. It seeks to uncover how demographic and socioeconomic factors affect these cancer mortality disparities for certain groups. The core research question is to investigate how these factors play a role in higher or lower life expectancy outcomes when it comes to cancer mortalities. This will be a project in which policymakers, public health workers, and healthcare providers can identify vulnerable groups that need targeted, high-impact resource allocation to mitigate the disparities. In order to answer this project’s research question, a variety of datasets are utilized in order to capture a high granularity of the U.S. beyond surface level trends:
-	CDC Multiple Cause of Death (2018-2020): Provides the high-resolution mortality outcomes necessary to identify recent trends.
-	National Cancer Institute County-Level Rates: Allows for geographic clustering analysis to identify regional outliers.
-	NCHS/KFF Life Expectancy: Provides the historical context needed to determine if current cancer disparities are part of a broader longitudinal trend.
-	Census Demographic Data: Enables risk-adjustment for age and sex, ensuring disparities aren't simply a reflection of local population distributions.
-	USDA Socioeconomic Indicators: Essential for isolating the impact of poverty and unemployment from clinical health factors.
